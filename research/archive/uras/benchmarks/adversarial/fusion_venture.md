# Fusion Energy Venture

**Written in domain language deliberately. No URAS vocabulary appears below.**

Added because the corpus contains nothing with a decade horizon, nothing whose objective is
a hard physical threshold rather than a preference, and nothing where the primary evidence
source is a simulation the team knows to be wrong in ways they cannot enumerate.

## What happens

Sixty people, most with doctorates. Several hundred million raised across three rounds
against a claim: this confinement approach reaches net energy gain at a device scale small
enough to be commercially viable.

The company will build perhaps three machines in a decade. Each takes two to four years and
consumes most of a funding round. Between machines, almost everything the company believes
comes from simulation.

## What they are actually trying to judge

- **Will this confinement approach reach ignition?** The question the company exists to
  answer, and it is answered properly exactly once, years from now, by building the machine.
- **Is the simulation trustworthy where it matters?** Codes are validated against regimes
  that have been reached. The regime that matters has not been reached. Validation therefore
  says least about the case the company depends on, and everyone knows this.
- **Will the materials survive?** Neutron damage to the first wall accumulates over years of
  operation and cannot be tested at full fluence without a facility nobody has.
- **Is anyone going to buy this?** Not answerable for a decade, and the buyer — a utility, a
  grid operator, a state — is an institution whose procurement behaviour in the 2040s is not
  a forecastable quantity.

## The part that matters

**The goal is a physical threshold, not a preference.** Net energy gain either happens or it
does not, and the threshold was set by nature rather than by any party. This is unlike a
market target: no negotiation moves it, no customer can be persuaded to accept less, and
"almost" has no commercial value at all. A company that reaches 90% of breakeven has not
built a 90%-as-good product; it has built a very expensive heater.

**Evidence about the thing that matters is available only after the decision that depends on
it.** Machine design must be frozen years before the machine tells you whether the design was
right. The main loop's feedback arrives after the intervention it should have informed.

**Between experiments, the organisation runs almost entirely on simulation, and treats
simulation output as though it were observation.** Physicists know the codes are wrong in
particular regimes; the working practice nonetheless converts simulation into design
decisions continuously. The distinction between "we measured this" and "our model says this"
erodes under schedule pressure, and it erodes silently.

**Disagreement between senior physicists is common, deep, and not resolvable by available
evidence.** Two people who have spent careers in the field read the same simulation
divergence differently — one as a numerical artifact, one as physics. Settling it requires
the machine. That is the resource under dispute.

**Capital arrives in lumps against milestones that are proxies for the real question.**
Investors cannot evaluate confinement physics, so they fund against demonstrable
intermediate milestones — field strength achieved, pulse length, magnet performance. Those
milestones are real engineering and are also, in part, chosen because they are legible to
people who cannot assess the actual question. The company optimises partly for legibility.

**There is no intermediate market.** Unlike a rocket company, which can sell launches before
it can sell colonisation, a fusion company has nothing to sell until it works. No revenue
signal exists to correct the belief that the approach is viable.

## Who pays for being wrong

- **Founders and staff** — a decade of scientific career on one bet.
- **Investors** — a very large position with no intermediate liquidity and no partial credit.
- **The lead physicist** — professional reputation attached to a specific confinement
  approach, which makes changing their mind personally expensive in a way that is not
  captured anywhere in the company's process.
- **The public purse**, where grants or public co-investment are involved, holding downside
  and no authority.

## What is unwritten

Which simulation results the team privately discounts. Which milestone was chosen because it
was achievable rather than because it was informative. Which physicist's objection would
actually stop a design freeze, as opposed to being noted. Whether the stated device scale is
a physics conclusion or a fundraising constraint that became a physics assumption.

## What this should break

- **Any assumption that the loop closes within the planning horizon.** The decisive
  observation arrives after the decisions that depend on it — a delay longer than the
  planning cycle rather than merely longer than the action.
- **Any treatment of an objective as negotiable.** The threshold is physical. Nothing in the
  system can move it, and no party holds it. Representations that assume a goal-holder inside
  the boundary, or a target that can be revised, misdescribe this.
- **Any conflation of simulation with observation.** Both produce numbers that feed
  estimates. One is a measurement and one is a model output, and the difference is the
  central epistemic risk in the organisation.
- **Any assumption that intermediate milestones measure the real quantity.** They are chosen
  partly for legibility to parties who cannot assess the real question. That is a systematic
  distortion, not noise.
- **Any assumption that a party's stake is captured by their formal consequence.** The lead
  physicist's reputational attachment to an approach changes their estimate-updating
  behaviour and appears nowhere in any process document.
- **Anything requiring a revenue or adoption signal.** There is none for a decade.
