## Representation, projection and reality

For Bhaskar:

### Representation

A representation is a fallible, socially produced account of something real.

It is neither a transparent mirror nor merely an arbitrary fabrication.



=====

That is one form of what Bhaskar calls the **epistemic fallacy**: reducing questions about being to questions about our knowledge of being. citeturn493057search2turn493057search4

## Bhaskar's three levels

His decisive contribution is the distinction between:

| Domain | Contains |
|---|---|
| **Empirical** | What is experienced or measured |
| **Actual** | Events that occur, observed or not |
| **Real** | Structures, mechanisms and causal powers that generate events |

So:



=====

A causal power can be real without being exercised. It can be exercised without producing its expected event because another mechanism counteracts it. And an event can occur without anyone observing it. citeturn493057search2turn493057search5

This is highly relevant to your distinction:

- **Representation** belongs primarily to the empirical and transitive domain.
- **Projection** mistakes properties of that representation for properties of the real.
- **Realist explanation** tries to infer the deeper generative mechanisms that make observed events possible.

## Relation to Deleuze and Guattari

Bhaskar and Deleuze–Guattari share more than it first appears.

They all reject:

- naïve representation as mirroring;
- flat empiricism;
- reduction of reality to observable events;
- simple linear causality;
- the idea that stable identities are philosophically primary.

They also share an interest in **emergence, stratification, powers and generative processes**.

But their emphases differ.

### Deleuze and Guattari

They ask:



=====

Deleuze and Guattari foreground the production of realities; Bhaskar foregrounds the reality of the mechanisms involved in that production.

Bhaskar would accept that institutions, subjectivities and categories are socially produced. But once produced, they can become **real causal structures** with effects not reducible to anyone's representation of them.

Money is socially constructed, for example, but a lack of money has real causal consequences.

### One way to summarize the historical progression

- **Leibniz:** Every perspective **expresses** the world.
- **Kant:** Representation organizes experience through the mind's categories.
- **Deleuze:** Representation suppresses genuine difference and becoming.
- **Deleuze 

=====

Guattari:** Replace representation with **production**—assemblages generate realities rather than merely depicting them.
- **Cybernetics:** Replace static representation with **operative models** embedded in feedback loops. Models exist to regulate, adapt, and transform, not just to describe.

This last step is one reason cybernetics sits so naturally alongside Deleuze and Guattari: both are interested less in *what the world is* than in *how systems continuously produce and transform worlds*.
