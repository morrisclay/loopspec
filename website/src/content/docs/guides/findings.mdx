A LoopSpec finding is a review object, not a verdict on the real system. It says what follows from
the encoded structure, how strong that inference is, what evidence supports shipping the check,
and what declaration would make the structure reviewable.

## Read the output in this order

```text
SPECIFIC
  [irreversible_without_approval] structural
    Action `publish_refund` cannot be undone and has no approval gate.
    repair: Add `needs_approval:` naming the person who must say yes.
```

<Steps>
1. **Pattern** — the stable machine identity, such as `irreversible_without_approval`.
2. **Assurance** — what kind of inference the represented data permits.
3. **Claim** — the exact structural fact found in this spec.
4. **Repair** — how to make the missing design decision explicit.
5. **Evidence status** — in JSON and the catalog, how well the check has survived corpus or
   independent-encoding scrutiny.
</Steps>

## Ranking is information, not severity

Findings are organized by how unusual they are in the repository corpus:

| Bucket | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `SPECIFIC` | rare in the current corpus; inspect first |
| `COMMON` | seen in a meaningful minority of loops |
| `UNIVERSAL CONTEXT` | widespread enough to carry little information about this individual loop |
| `CONSIDERED` | still present, with an explicit design decision attached |

A universal pattern may matter greatly about the field while distinguishing your loop hardly at
all. Base-rate ranking prevents common omissions from drowning out unusual ones; it does not say
rare means dangerous.

## Assurance and evidence answer different questions

<Tabs>
	<TabItem label="Assurance">
**What can this finding establish?** Current authoring checks are structural or qualitative
proxies. Quantitative and empirical labels are reserved for represented models or observed
outcomes that do not yet exist in the current language.
	</TabItem>
	<TabItem label="Evidence status">
**Why is this check shipped?** `robust` survived independent encoding, `corpus` is measured in
the repository corpus, `motivated` has concrete cases but no stable prevalence estimate, and
`legacy` is compatibility-only.
	</TabItem>
</Tabs>

<Aside type="caution" title="A theorem citation is not a theorem result">
`no_explicit_process_model` is motivated by the Good Regulator theorem but establishes only that
no explicit explanatory relation was encoded. `unmeasured_estimand` is a sensing-path check, not
Kalman observability. `target_without_actuator` is an actuation-path check, not controllability.
</Aside>

## The resolution loop

For every finding:

1. Return to the source or designer and verify that the encoding is faithful.
2. Decide whether the structural omission is real.
3. Repair the spec if the design contains the missing semantics.
4. Repair the design if the omission is consequential.
5. Record `consider:` when the finding is correct but deliberately accepted.

```yaml
consider:
  belief_never_checked/product_market_fit:
    because: no outcome arrives before the current design decision must be made
    revisit: after the first six-month retention cohort resolves
```

The complete generated [check catalog](/reference/checks/) gives the felt symptom, inspection,
repair, assurance, evidence status, and a real corpus example for every check.

## Machine-readable review

```bash
loopspec check system.loop.yaml --json > review.json
```

JSON includes the exact finding records and is the correct surface for automation. Use
`--fail-on-findings` only after your team has defined what unresolved findings are allowed to
cross a review boundary.
