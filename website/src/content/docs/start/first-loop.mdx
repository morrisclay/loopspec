You will build a support-triage loop. The first version is intentionally incomplete: the point
is to make omissions discussable, not to make a linter green on the first try.

## Begin with five questions

<Steps>
1. **What condition is the loop trying to regulate?**
2. **What information actually arrives?**
3. **What can it do, and through what process does that matter?**
4. **What rule connects the information to the action?**
5. **Who drew this boundary, can interrupt the loop, and bears the loss?**
</Steps>

If nothing comes back around on a later pass, stop: you have a pipeline, not a loop.

## Write the smallest honest draft

Create `support.loop.yaml`:

```yaml title="support.loop.yaml"
loop: support_triage
runs: per_ticket

boundary:
  drawn_by: support_lead
  purpose: route urgent support work without losing customer trust
  inside: [triage policy, support queue]
  outside: [customer situation, product behavior]

goal:
  resolution_time:
    keep: below 4 hours

beliefs:
  ticket_severity:
    question: "How badly is this customer blocked?"
    how: judgement

observes:
  ticket_text:
    informs: ticket_severity
    origin: outside
    how: reported

processes:
  support_workflow:
    location: inside
    observed_as: []

actions:
  escalate_to_engineer:
    moves: resolution_time
    through: support_workflow
    effect: decrease
    can_undo: yes

when:
  - if: ticket_severity is high
    reads: [ticket_severity]
    do: escalate_to_engineer

people:
  support_lead:
    human: yes
    loses_if_wrong: the customer relationship
    sees: [ticket_severity]
```

Two distinctions matter immediately:

- `origin` asks whether something outside the modeled system caused the data to exist.
- `how` asks whether the value was measured, reported, calculated, or judged.

A customer ticket is both `origin: outside` and `how: reported`. An agent grading its own output
is `origin: ourselves` and `how: reported`. Collapsing those axes hides the case that most needs
review.

## Lint it

```bash
loopspec check support.loop.yaml
```

The first findings should include these structural gaps:

| Finding | Why it appears |
|---|---|
| `unmeasured_estimand` | the loop targets resolution time but nothing observes it |
| `belief_never_checked` | ticket severity is judged but never scored against a later outcome |
| `reference_not_used` | no rule compares observed resolution time with its four-hour target |
| `no_escalation_path` | a person exists, but no condition stops and asks them |

<Aside type="tip" title="Do not optimize for zero findings">
A good result is a spec whose remaining findings have been read and decided about. A finding can
be correct and still not justify a change in your context.
</Aside>

## Close the missing paths

Add the later outcome, the calibration contract, the reference-using rule, a resource, and an
escalation recipient:

```yaml
beliefs:
  ticket_severity:
    question: "How badly is this customer blocked?"
    how: judgement
    explains: resolution_time
    checked_by: weekly_severity_review
    checked_against: ticket_outcome
    scoring_rule: severity classification error
    window: 100 resolved tickets
    adjusts: trust
    every: weekly

observes:
  ticket_outcome:
    informs: [ticket_severity, resolution_time]
    origin: outside
    how: measured

processes:
  support_workflow:
    location: inside
    observed_as: [ticket_outcome]

when:
  - if: ticket_severity is high
    reads: [ticket_severity]
    do: escalate_to_engineer
  - if: resolution_time exceeds 4 hours
    reads: [resolution_time]
    against: [resolution_time]
    do: escalate_to_engineer

spends:
  engineer_hours:
    limit: 8 per week
    spent_by: [escalate_to_engineer]

asks_human_when: [severity evidence conflicts]
asks_human: support_lead
```

## Record a deliberate exception

Do not delete or suppress a finding you have consciously accepted. Record the decision:

```yaml
consider:
  expensive_signal_unreviewed/customer_interviews:
    because: retained for quarterly qualitative discovery, not a policy input
    revisit: when interview hours exceed the research budget
```

The finding moves to `CONSIDERED`. If a later edit means it no longer fires, LoopSpec reports the
stale justification.
